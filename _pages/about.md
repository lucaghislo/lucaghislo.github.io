I am a Research Fellow at the Pavia division of the [Italian National Institute for Nuclear Physics (INFN)](https://home.infn.it/en/), working within the CSN2 astroparticle physics group.  
I received my **Ph.D. in Engineering and Applied Sciences** from the [University of Bergamo](https://en.unibg.it/) in January 2026, and I continue my research at its [Microelectronics Laboratory](https://microlab.unibg.it/#/home).

I design and characterize low-noise, low-power analog integrated circuits for the readout of silicon detectors in high-energy physics and astrophysics experiments. My work covers the whole path from transistor-level design in deep-submicron CMOS to test boards and experimental characterization in the lab.

Since 2022 I have been a member of the [General AntiParticle Spectrometer](https://gaps1.astro.ucla.edu/gaps/index.html) (GAPS) collaboration. GAPS is a NASA/JAXA/INFN/ASI Antarctic balloon experiment and the first optimized to detect low-energy cosmic-ray antinuclei: it measures the flux of antideuterons, antiprotons and antihelium as an indirect signature of dark matter. I worked on the testing and calibration of the Si(Li) tracker readout electronics, from integration at UC Berkeley, Columbia University and NASA facilities to the 2024 Antarctic campaign at McMurdo Station. GAPS completed its first flight in December 2025, staying aloft for 25 days. I am now designing the 65 nm CMOS readout ASIC for the tracker upgrade of the second flight.
